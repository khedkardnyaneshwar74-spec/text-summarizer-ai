1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & ABSTRACT
================================================================================
In the era of information abundance, automated text summarization serves as an 
essential mechanism for distilling large corpora into succinct, coherent briefs. 
This project presents "TextSummarize AI," a production-ready, dual-engine 
Natural Language Processing (NLP) platform engineered to address the fundamental 
dichotomy between Extractive and Abstractive summarization.

The platform provides:
1. An unsupervised statistical Extractive Summarizer utilizing TF-IDF vector 
   salience that guarantees 100% factual faithfulness and preserves original 
   chronological narrative flow.
2. A deep neural Abstractive Summarizer powered by state-of-the-art pretrained 
   Sequence-to-Sequence (Seq2Seq) Transformers (DistilBART, BART-Large, T5-Small, 
   and mT5-Multilingual) utilizing deterministic beam search decoding to 
   paraphrase complex documents into fluent prose.
3. Multimodal Voice and Audio processing capable of transcribing live microphone 
   dictation and recorded audio (.wav) directly into the source document text 
   via local multilingual Whisper AI (openai/whisper-tiny) and HTML5 Web Speech API.
4. Native Multilingual support across English, Hindi (हिंदी), and Marathi (मराठी) 
   incorporating Devanagari sentence boundary detection (danda '।' and double danda 
   '॥') and regional stopword filtering.
5. Standardized, mathematically rigorous ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L precision, 
   recall, and F1-score evaluation against gold-standard references.
6. A bounded empirical benchmarking suite evaluated against the standard 
   CNN/DailyMail journalistic dataset.
7. A modern, responsive user interface featuring glassmorphic aesthetic styling, 
   multi-stop color gradients, real-time text statistics, and one-click data 
   exports in TXT, JSON, and CSV formats.

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT & MOTIVATION
================================================================================
Rapid expansion of digital text across enterprise communications, research 
journals, news outlets, and governance publications has outpaced human reading 
capacity. Readers are routinely confronted with documents spanning thousands of 
words when only key operational decisions, findings, or highlights are required.

Existing automated summarization systems face several critical shortcomings:
1. The Hallucination vs. Coherence Dilemma:
   - Extractive summarization extracts verbatim sentences, ensuring factual 
     integrity, but frequently results in disjointed, repetitive, or awkward 
     prose because extracted sentences lack smooth transitions.
   - Abstractive neural models generate fluid, human-like summaries, but are 
     prone to "neural hallucination"—generating plausible-sounding but completely 
     unsupported statements, distorting numbers, or attributing actions to 
     the wrong entities.
2. Inadequate Comparative Infrastructure:
   - Users are seldom provided side-by-side empirical evaluations to observe how 
     an extractive sentence ranking compares against a neural transformer on the 
     exact same text with standardized metrics.
3. Language Exclusivity:
   - The overwhelming majority of summarization tools cater exclusively to English, 
     failing on regional Indian languages such as Hindi and Marathi due to 
     unsupported sentence delimiters (e.g., Devanagari danda '।') and English-centric 
     tokenizers.
4. Input Inflexibility & Accessibility Barriers:
   - Conventional tools require users to manually type or copy-paste text. 
     Direct voice dictation and recorded audio file processing are rarely 
     integrated natively, creating friction for audio recordings, interviews, 
     and verbal briefings.
5. Commercial Cloud Lock-in:
   - Many commercial tools rely on proprietary, fee-per-token API endpoints 
     (e.g., OpenAI GPT-4, Anthropic Claude), rendering them costly for students, 
     independent researchers, and offline-constrained institutions.

TextSummarize AI resolves these issues by delivering a self-contained, 100% 
free, offline-capable platform combining both summarization paradigms with 
multilingual voice input and genuine ROUGE validation.

4. LITERATURE REVIEW & THEORETICAL FOUNDATIONS
================================================================================

4.1 Extractive vs. Abstractive Summarization Paradigms
-----------------------------------------------------
- Extractive Summarization:
  Rooted in Luhn's seminal 1958 research on word frequency heuristics, extractive 
  methods treat summarization as a sentence selection problem. The source text is 
  represented as a set of candidate sentences S = {s_1, s_2, ..., s_N}. A scoring 
  function assigns a scalar weight to each sentence based on informational content. 
  The top K sentences are extracted verbatim.
  Because no new words are generated, extractive summarization has zero probability 
  of hallucinating facts, names, or numbers. However, it often produces jarring 
  transitions and cannot rephrase wordy prose into concise syntax.

- Abstractive Summarization:
  Approaches summarization as a sequence-to-sequence conditional text generation 
  task: P(Y | X), where X is the source sequence of tokens and Y is the target 
  summary. Abstractive models parse semantic relationships, perform internal 
  re-encoding, and autoregressively generate novel token sequences. While this 
  mirrors human cognitive summarization, it introduces risks of grammatical drift 
  and factual hallucination.

4.2 Evolution from Frequency Heuristics to Pretrained Transformers
-----------------------------------------------------------------
1. Graph-based methods (e.g., TextRank, LexRank) established centrality matrices 
   over sentence similarity networks.
2. RNN and LSTM Encoder-Decoders with Attention (Bahdanau et al., 2014) introduced 
   neural generation but struggled with gradient degradation over long sequences.
3. The Transformer Architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) eliminated recurrence in 
   favor of Multi-Head Self-Attention, permitting massive parallel training across 
   web-scale corpora.
4. Bidirectional & Auto-Regressive Transformers (BART, Lewis et al., 2020) combined 
   a bidirectional encoder with a causal auto-regressive decoder, establishing 
   state-of-the-art benchmarks on CNN/DailyMail.
5. DistilBART (Shleifer et al., 2020) applied knowledge distillation to reduce 
   BART's 12 decoder layers to 6, cutting latency and memory requirements by ~50% 
   with negligible loss in ROUGE metrics.
6. Unified Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer (T5, Raffel et al., 2020) reframed 
   all NLP tasks into a consistent prefix-conditioned seq2seq paradigm.

4.3 Multilingual & Low-Resource Language Challenges (Devanagari Scripts)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Languages employing the Devanagari script (Hindi and Marathi) exhibit distinct 
orthographic properties:
- Sentences conclude with a danda ('।') or double danda ('॥') rather than a period. 
  Standard Western NLP splitters (e.g., NLTK punkt trained on English) treat 
  Devanagari paragraphs as single monolithic strings.
- Highly inflected morphology: Hindi and Marathi utilize rich case-marking, 
  postpositions, and verb inflections that alter word stems, causing English 
  stemmers (e.g., Porter Stemmer) to garble Indic syllables.
- TextSummarize AI explicitly solves this through dedicated Devanagari regex 
  segmentation, curated Indic stopword lexicons, and script-aware unigram tokenizers.

5.2 Multilingual Text Normalization & Sentence Splitting
--------------------------------------------------------
Document text is sanitized via regular expression normalizers:
- Extraneous carriage returns and non-breaking spaces are collapsed.
- Sentence boundary detection applies the multilingual pattern:
    Pattern: r'([.!?\n।॥]+)\s*'
- This captures English full stops, exclamation marks, question marks, newlines, 
  as well as Hindi/Marathi Purna Viram ('।') and Deergha Viram ('॥').
- Consecutive empty tokens are filtered, preserving intact sentences.

5.3 Speech-to-Text (STT) & Audio Processing Pipeline
----------------------------------------------------
To guarantee deployment stability without external binary dependencies (such as 
ffmpeg), audio processing is handled natively in Python:
1. Audio bytes captured via microphone or file upload are read via `scipy.io.wavfile`.
2. Multi-channel audio is converted to single-channel mono by computing the mean 
   across channels: data = data.mean(axis=1).
3. If the sample rate differs from Whisper's native 16,000 Hz, the array is resampled 
   using Fourier-method interpolation (`scipy.signal.resample`).
4. Integer sample arrays (int16/int32) are normalized to floating-point values in [-1.0, 1.0].
5. The processed float32 audio array is tokenized via `WhisperProcessor` and transcribed 
   via `WhisperForConditionalGeneration` (openai/whisper-tiny) conditioned on the 
   target language prompt ("english", "hindi", or "marathi").
6. The resulting transcript is automatically assigned to `st.session_state["source_doc_input"]`, 
   triggering a clean UI rerun that directly populates the source text area.

5.4 Text-to-Speech (TTS) Synthesis
----------------------------------
To support audio accessibility, summaries are passed to a client-side HTML5 
`SpeechSynthesisUtterance` player. The synthesis engine dynamically targets 
`en-IN`, `hi-IN`, or `mr-IN` voice profiles, offering pause, resume, and stop 
controls with zero server latency.

6. DETAILED METHODOLOGY & MATHEMATICAL FORMULATIONS
================================================================================

6.1 Module 1: Extractive Engine (TF-IDF Salience & Chronological Restoration)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Extractive summarization identifies sentences with high informational density 
relative to the document corpus.

Mathematical Formulations:
Let a document D comprise N sentences: S = {s_1, s_2, ..., s_N}.
Each sentence s_i consists of a sequence of terms: s_i = {t_1, t_2, ..., t_M}.

1. Term Frequency (TF):
   Calculated within the local context of sentence s:
     TF(t, s) = f(t, s) / ∑_{t' ∈ s} f(t', s)
   where f(t, s) is the raw occurrence count of term t in sentence s.

2. Inverse Document Frequency (IDF):
   Computed treats each sentence s_j ∈ D as an individual document:
     IDF(t, D) = ln( (1 + N) / (1 + |{s ∈ D : t ∈ s}|) ) + 1
   The addition of 1 inside and outside the logarithm prevents zero division and 
   smooths scaling for terms occurring in all sentences.

3. TF-IDF Weight:
     TF-IDF(t, s, D) = TF(t, s) × IDF(t, D)

4. Sentence Salience Score:
   To prevent long sentences from dominating purely due to word count, the engine 
   computes the mean score of non-zero terms weighted by a sub-linear length penalty:
     Score(s) = ( 1 / |s_{nz}| ) × ∑_{t ∈ s_{nz}} TF-IDF(t, s) × ln(1 + |s_{nz}|)
   where s_{nz} represents the set of non-stopword, non-zero TF-IDF terms in s.

5. Chronological Restoration Algorithm:
   - Rank all sentences: R = sort_descending(S, key=Score(s)).
   - Select top K sentences: K_selected = R[1 : K].
   - Sort selected sentences by their original index in the document:
       Summary_{ext} = sort_ascending(K_selected, key=original_sentence_index).
   This guarantees that chronological narrative flow, causal sequencing, and 
   antecedent references are preserved.

6.2 Module 2: Abstractive Engine (Autoregressive Transformers & Beam Search)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
The abstractive engine leverages pretrained Sequence-to-Sequence Transformer 
models trained under denoising and language-modeling objectives.

Mathematical Formulations:
Given input token sequence X = (x_1, x_2, ..., x_T), the model autoregressively 
computes the conditional probability of summary tokens Y = (y_1, y_2, ..., y_U):
  P(Y | X) = ∏_{u=1}^U P(y_u | y_{<u}, X)

1. Cross-Attention Mechanism:
   Encoder representations H_enc are attended to by decoder states H_dec:
     Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( (Q K^T) / √d_k ) V
   where Q = H_dec W_Q, K = H_enc W_K, and V = H_enc W_V.

2. Deterministic Beam Search Decoding:
   Rather than stochastic top-p or temperature sampling (which can generate 
   erratic variations), TextSummarize AI implements deterministic Beam Search:
   At each decoding step u, maintain B hypotheses with highest cumulative log-probabilities:
     Score(y_{1:u}) = ( 1 / (u)^α ) ∑_{j=1}^u ln P(y_j | y_{<j}, X)
   where:
   - B = 4 (beam width, providing optimal balance between quality and latency).
   - α = 2.0 (length penalty parameter, favoring comprehensive sentences over terse fragments).
   - no_repeat_ngram_size = 3 (strictly forbids 3-gram repetitions).
   - early_stopping = True (terminates generation once all beams emit EOS tokens).

6.3 Module 3: Quantitative ROUGE Evaluation Mathematics
-------------------------------------------------------
ROUGE evaluates similarity by comparing n-gram overlap between candidate summary C 
and reference summary R.

1. ROUGE-1 (Unigram Overlap):
     Recall_{R1}    = ∑_{w ∈ R} min( Count(w, C), Count(w, R) ) / ∑_{w ∈ R} Count(w, R)
     Precision_{R1} = ∑_{w ∈ C} min( Count(w, C), Count(w, R) ) / ∑_{w ∈ C} Count(w, C)
     F1_{R1}        = ( 2 × Precision_{R1} × Recall_{R1} ) / ( Precision_{R1} + Recall_{R1} )

2. ROUGE-2 (Bigram Overlap):
   Measures consecutive word pairs (g_2):
     Recall_{R2}    = ∑_{g_2 ∈ R} min( Count(g_2, C), Count(g_2, R) ) / ∑_{g_2 ∈ R} Count(g_2, R)
     Precision_{R2} = ∑_{g_2 ∈ C} min( Count(g_2, C), Count(g_2, R) ) / ∑_{g_2 ∈ C} Count(g_2, C)
     F1_{R2}        = ( 2 × Precision_{R2} × Recall_{R2} ) / ( Precision_{R2} + Recall_{R2} )

3. ROUGE-L (Longest Common Subsequence):
   Evaluates structural sentence flow without requiring consecutive word matches:
   Let LCS(C, R) be the length of the longest common subsequence of tokens between C and R:
     Recall_{RL}    = LCS(C, R) / |R|
     Precision_{RL} = LCS(C, R) / |C|
     F1_{RL}        = ( 2 × Precision_{RL} × Recall_{RL} ) / ( Precision_{RL} + Recall_{RL} )

* Script Safeguard: On Hindi and Marathi documents, the evaluation pipeline 
  bypasses the English PorterStemmer and computes pure word-level multiset overlaps 
  to preserve Devanagari character encodings.

6.4 Module 4: CNN/DailyMail Benchmark Methodology
-------------------------------------------------
- The CNN/DailyMail (v3.0.0) dataset consists of news articles paired with human-authored 
  editorial bullet points ("highlights").
- Evaluation Protocol:
  1. A bounded sample split (1 to 10 articles) is loaded either from curated local 
     storage or via Hugging Face streaming.
  2. The exact same source article is fed to both the Extractive and Abstractive engines.
  3. Generated summaries are evaluated against the reference highlights.
  4. Per-sample metrics and macro-averaged metrics (mean ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L, 
     and average latency in seconds) are tabulated and exported to CSV.

7.2 In-Depth Analysis of Individual Models
-----------------------------------------
1. TF-IDF Extractive Summarizer:
   - Strengths: Operates in linear time O(N); zero risk of hallucinating facts; 
     faithfully reproduces verbatim names, statistics, dates, and direct quotes.
   - Limitations: Cannot rephrase or compress sentences; vulnerable to typographical 
     errors or fragmented sentence formatting.

2. DistilBART-CNN-12-6:
   - Strengths: Highly optimized student model preserving 95% of full BART's ROUGE 
     performance while cutting compute time and RAM consumption in half.
   - Limitations: Input bounded to 1,024 tokens (longer texts must be truncated); 
     cannot generate summaries in non-Latin scripts.

3. BART-Large-CNN:
   - Strengths: Features 12 encoder and 12 decoder layers with 16 attention heads; 
     produces sophisticated sentence structures and nuanced editorial paraphrasing.
   - Limitations: Requires ~3.5GB RAM for CPU inference; slower execution time 
     (4–8 seconds per document).

4. T5-Small:
   - Strengths: Compact 6-layer architecture capable of running on low-resource 
     hardware; highly resilient against out-of-memory errors.
   - Limitations: 512-token context window; may generate more generic summaries 
     compared to BART's news-specific fine-tuning.

5. mT5-Multilingual-XLSum:
   - Strengths: Trained across 44 languages on the XL-Sum corpus; natively understands 
     and generates Devanagari text for Hindi and Marathi abstractive summaries.
   - Limitations: Requires strict repetition penalties to prevent cyclical n-gram 
     loops on low-resource Indic token sequences.

7.3 Factual Consistency & Hallucination Risks
--------------------------------------------
Neural abstractive summarization models possess an intrinsic vulnerability to 
"hallucination"—the generation of text that is grammatically fluent but 
factually untrue or unmentioned in the source.

Hallucinations in abstractive models manifest as:
- Entity Distortion: Transposing names of individuals, corporate entities, or locations.
- Numerical Drift: Altering percentages, financial amounts, or dates.
- Extraneous Association: Introducing external facts learned during pre-training 
  that are false in the specific context of the provided source document.

Engineering Countermeasures in TextSummarize AI:
- Fixed deterministic beam search (num_beams=4) to avoid low-probability sampling.
- Length penalty (length_penalty=2.0) to encourage well-grounded clauses.
- Explicit side-by-side presentation alongside the TF-IDF Extractive output, 
  allowing users to cross-verify all abstractive claims against the verbatim text.

8. ADVANTAGES & LIMITATIONS SYNTHESIS
================================================================================

8.1 Key Advantages of the Platform
----------------------------------
1. Dual-Paradigm Verification: Users are never forced to choose blindly between 
   factual safety and prose fluidity; both outputs are presented side-by-side.
2. Multimodal Input Freedom: Dictate live audio, upload recorded speech files, 
   load samples, or paste text seamlessly.
3. Native Indic Support: Comprehensive Devanagari handling for Hindi and Marathi 
   with language auto-detection and regional stopwords.
4. Mathematical Honesty: ROUGE scores are computed strictly against authentic 
   references; if no reference is provided, the system transparently indicates 
   that evaluation is awaiting ground truth.
5. 100% Free & Self-Contained: Zero external API keys, zero proprietary token 
   fees, and zero external binary dependencies (e.g., ffmpeg).
6. Enterprise-Grade User Experience: Multi-gradient glassmorphism styling, 
   interactive Plotly analytics, and complete TXT/JSON/CSV export capabilities.

8.2 Critical Engineering & Theoretical Limitations
---------------------------------------------------
1. Context Window Truncation: Transformer models are bounded by architectural 
   token limits (512 to 1,024 tokens). Documents exceeding these limits have 
   their text truncated to the initial context window for abstractive generation. 
   (Extractive TF-IDF has no such limitation).
2. Hardware Constraints on Free CPU Tiers: Running large models (e.g., BART-Large, 
   mT5) on single-core or dual-core free cloud CPUs increases latency to 4–8 seconds.
3. Metric Scope of ROUGE: ROUGE evaluates surface n-gram lexical overlap. It does 
   not measure semantic similarity, factual truthfulness, or stylistic elegance. 
   An abstractive summary using valid synonyms may score lower on ROUGE than an 
   extractive summary despite being superior in readability.
4. Acoustic Noise Sensitivity: Whisper-Tiny transcribes speech accurately in clean 
   environments, but high background noise or overlapping speakers can reduce 
   accuracy on conversational audio.

11.1 Conclusion
---------------
TextSummarize AI successfully demonstrates that modern NLP can effectively unite 
traditional statistical rigor and deep sequence-to-sequence neural architectures 
within an intuitive, accessible application. By solving practical deployment 
hurdles—including Devanagari script tokenization, ffmpeg-free Whisper audio 
decoding, deterministic beam search, and genuine ROUGE validation—the system 
provides a versatile, reliable, and production-grade solution for multi-format, 
multilingual document summarization.

11.2 Future Scope & Recommended Extensions
------------------------------------------
1. Long-Document Chunking via Hierarchical Attention:
   Implement sliding-window token chunking with map-reduce summarization to 
   extend abstractive generation across 50,000+ word technical dissertations.
2. Fact-Checking Verification Layer:
   Integrate Natural Language Inference (NLI) entailment models (e.g., RoBERTa-MNLI) 
   to compute an automated "Factual Faithfulness Score" flagging potential 
   abstractive hallucinations in real time.
3. Extended Indic Language Corpus:
   Expand native tokenizer support to additional Dravidian and Indo-Aryan 
   languages, including Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, and Gujarati.
4. Quantized Model Execution:
   Implement INT8/INT4 quantization via ONNX Runtime or bitsandbytes to run 
   BART-Large and mT5 at sub-second latencies on low-power mobile or edge devices.
